## Chosen

**#1 — 102 characters, 104 bytes**

> At 2:47 a.m. I skipped that spot — Gerald the dust bunny had unionized the crumbs and seized the dock.

Why it won: the only candidate to carry all three winning ingredients
at once — a named person (Gerald), a specific time (2:47 a.m.), and a
consequence that flips the premise in the very last words (the dirt
seizes the dock, the vacuum's own home base). Labor-union escalation
plus a hostage twist is the most absurd of the five, and every word
lives in vacuum-world (dust bunny, crumbs, dock).

## Rejected

**#2 — 99 characters, 105 bytes**

> I didn’t skip that spot — I adopted it. The dust bunny’s name is Gerald and he pays rent in crumbs.

A strong reframe ("adopted it", "pays rent in crumbs") with a named
bunny, but no specific time, and the punchline ("in crumbs") is a cute
image rather than a consequence that turns the premise around. Weaker
escalation than #1's seized dock. Close second.

**#3 — 92 characters, 94 bytes**

> I left the dust bunny by the rug — it introduced itself as Kevin and asked for one more day.

Named person (Kevin) and a twist in the last words, but "asked for one
more day" is a polite request, not a reversal — the dust does nothing
to the vacuum. Lowest stakes of the five; reads as a cute observation,
which criterion 2 of the judge brief punishes.

**#4 — 106 characters, 110 bytes**

> The crumbs under the table weren’t dirt — they were my retirement fund, and I already spent it on the cat.

A strong collision (retirement fund / crumbs), but the payoff ("spent
it on the cat") is random rather than earned: the cat plays no role in
the setup, and nothing flips back onto the vacuum or the spot. The joke
explains itself instead of landing.

**#5 — 103 characters, 103 bytes**

> I missed the spot by the door because the rug whispered my serial number and I have never felt so seen.

Absurd and vivid, but the twist ("never felt so seen") is a feeling,
not an act — no consequence, no second concrete image colliding in the
last words. Closer to a cute observation than a joke; the setup
("because the rug whispered") also spends the surprise before the
punchline arrives.
